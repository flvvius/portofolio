# Seven decisions and why

1. Lead with the buyer's app. The original hero described me as an engineer. The new opening says what I improve, how I deliver it and what to do next. This follows the shared principle to begin with the person and task.
2. Give type distinct jobs. Inter carries the offer, scope, prices and terms. Schoolbell is reserved for annotations. The old code used Schoolbell everywhere, despite the README listing three other fonts.
3. Keep the content on the page. Pricing is two hairline rows; process and scope use alignment and spacing. Borders belong to inputs and media frames. This applies Editorial calm's rule that a section is not a surface.
4. Move the personality into the evidence. Handwritten marks point at screen decisions, with one small hero underline. The shelf drawings remain on About beside ordinary project links. A buyer never needs to decode an illustrated object to find work or prices.
5. State the boundary before someone pays. The audit is a walkthrough, ten ranked fixes and one screen preview. A sprint is implementation in PRs. The audit does not promise a production merge or business outcomes.
6. Preserve the portfolio and its links. Projects, experience, hackathon stories, education, writing and interests have a new home on About. Old fragments resolve there, and the back-room route sends an explicit 301 without losing its collection fragment.
7. Make missing evidence visible. The homepage pairs are marked placeholders. Pricele has a current local capture, not an invented historical comparison. Draft concepts stay out of production and testimonials render nothing. Missing checkout and delivery settings get truthful unavailable states.
